A note before you tour

The venue comes first

The venue is the first big decision you make, and nearly every decision after it depends on it.

Your guest count, your menu, your décor, your timeline, even the way your guests arrive: all of it has to fit inside the rules of the room you choose. A venue can be breathtaking on a tour and still be unable to support the celebration you are planning.

This checklist holds the same questions our planners ask on every venue tour. Take it with you, ask every question that applies, and do not pay a deposit until the answers that matter are in writing.

If your celebration brings together more than one culture, more than one ceremony, or traditions with specific needs — sacred fire, a procession, cultural catering, attire changes — the venue matters even more. The final chapter is written for you.

With care, Irene

Irene Ntiako · Founder & Creative Director

The one rule

“We’re flexible” is not a yes

Here is how it usually happens. On the tour, you mention that your caterer will cook on-site, and the coordinator smiles and says, “We’re very flexible.” Months later, the contract arrives, and it says outside food must arrive fully prepared. Nothing was promised in writing, so nothing was promised.

Before you sign

If a promise affects your ceremony, décor, food, music, parking, guest count, budget, installation, or timeline, request written confirmation in the contract.

How to use this checklist

Four steps, one tour at a time

  1. 01
    Before the tourRead it through and cross out anything that does not apply to your wedding.
  2. 02
    On the tourAsk each question. Write down the answer and the name of the person who gave it.
  3. 03
    After the tourAsk the venue to confirm the important answers by email or in the contract. You don’t need every answer confirmed, just the ones that affect your plans.
  4. 04
    Before you signAnything not confirmed in writing becomes a change to request in the contract, or a reason to keep looking.

AskedTick when you have asked the question and noted the answer.

ConfirmedTick when the venue confirms the answer by email or in the contract.

What to bring to the tour

  • Your guest count, and the most it could grow to
  • Your wedding date, or the dates you are considering
  • Your budget range for the venue
  • Photos of your design inspiration
  • A list of your ceremonies, traditions, and must-haves
  • A tape measure, and your phone for photos and video
  • This checklist and a pen
  • Your planner or designer, if you have one

01Date, Hours & Curfew

Setup, the celebration, and cleanup all come out of the same rental hours.

AskedConfirmed
  • What time may vendors begin load-in, and when must setup be complete?
  • What time must music, alcohol service, guest activity, and vendor load-out end?
  • What overtime fees apply to event time, staff, security, cleanup, or late strike?
  • Is there a local noise ordinance or amplified-sound limit that ends outdoor music earlier than the venue curfew?

02Real Capacity & Layout

The capacity on the brochure is usually for an empty room. Your dance floor, stage, bars, and décor all take seats away.

AskedConfirmed
  • What is the realistic seated capacity with tables, stage, dance floor, entertainment, bars, food stations, lounge furniture, and cultural structures?
  • Can the full guest count fit in the weather-backup space?
  • If weather forces a change, by what time must the call be made, and are tents, sidewalls, heaters, or ground flooring permitted?
  • Does capacity change for multiple ceremonies or room flips?

Notes

03Alcohol, Bar & Certificates

Whoever holds the license decides who pours, who is insured, and when the bar closes.

AskedConfirmed
  • Does the venue hold the required liquor license, or is a temporary permit needed?
  • Who obtains the permit, licensed bartenders, security, liquor-liability coverage, and certificate of insurance?
  • Must the venue be named as an additional insured?
  • What time must alcohol service end, and what bar fees apply?

04Catering & Kitchen Rules

If your caterer cannot cook the way your menu needs, the meal you are picturing may not be possible here.

AskedConfirmed
  • Is catering exclusive, preferred-list only, or open to outside cultural caterers?
  • May caterers cook on-site, and are tandoors, grills, fryers, woks, propane, refrigeration, water, ice, and waste disposal permitted?
  • Can halal, kosher, Jain, vegetarian, vegan, allergy, fasting, and cross-contact requirements be supported?

Notes

05In-House Rentals

“Included” can mean anything. Ask exactly what, how many, and in what condition.

AskedConfirmed
  • Which tables, chairs, linens, tabletop items, bars, staging, dance floors, lounge pieces, and cocktail tables are included?
  • What quantities, sizes, colors, and conditions are guaranteed?
  • Are delivery, setup, breakdown, cleaning, reset, and damage fees included?
  • May the dance floor be wrapped or covered with a custom vinyl wrap or overlay? What adhesive, installation, removal, and floor-protection rules apply, and who is liable for damage to the floor beneath?

06Décor Setup, Load-In & Breakdown Access

Your design is only as good as the hours, doors, and elevators you get to install it.

AskedConfirmed
  • Exactly how many hours are allowed for décor setup before guest arrival?
  • Exactly how many hours are allowed for décor breakdown and load-out?
  • Can installation begin the day before, and may décor remain overnight?
  • Is the space on the same level as the loading entrance?
  • Is there a loading dock, same-level entry, ramp, or service entrance for large pieces?
  • What are the freight-elevator interior dimensions, door opening, weight limit, and reservation rules?
  • Can oversized backdrops, stages, floral installations, carts, and rental furniture fit through every door, hallway, turn, and elevator?

07Power, Rigging & Effects

Lighting, music, chandeliers, and first-dance effects all need power and permission, and both run out.

AskedConfirmed
  • How much electrical power is available for lighting, entertainment, catering, and production, and where are the circuits and outlets located?
  • Are dedicated circuits, a power drop, or a generator required or provided, and what do they cost?
  • May chandeliers, florals, draping, or installations be hung from the ceiling? What rigging points, weight limits, and rigger requirements apply?
  • Are cold-spark machines permitted for entrances and first-dance moments, and what fire-marshal, insurance, or venue approvals apply?
  • Is a low-lying cloud effect (dry ice) permitted for the first dance? What rules apply to dry-ice delivery, storage, and handling, ventilation, and fire-alarm or smoke-detector sensitivity?
  • Is atmospheric haze permitted for lighting and photography effects?
  • If the venue loses power, is there backup power, and what happens to the event?

08Guest Parking, Valet & Arrival

Arrival is your guests’ first impression. A long walk in the rain is hard to undo.

AskedConfirmed
  • How many guest parking spaces are available?
  • Is parking complimentary, validated, or paid by guests?
  • Is valet required or optional, and what are the fee, minimum, staffing, hours, and gratuity rules?
  • Where are the guest drop-off, accessible drop-off, shuttle, bus, limousine, and rideshare areas?
  • Is overflow parking available, and is a shuttle required?
  • Can vendor vehicles park separately from guests?
  • Are there garage height limits, operating hours, traffic restrictions, or overnight-parking rules?
  • Is the path from parking safe, accessible, and well lit in bad weather?
  • Can a cultural procession occur without blocking valet, guest parking, traffic, or emergency access?

09Total Cost & Contract

The quote is rarely the total. Every fee you do not ask about shows up later.

AskedConfirmed
  • Does the quote include rental, service charges, taxes, staffing, security, cleaning, rentals, parking, valet, power, elevators, docks, overtime, and damage deposits?
  • Does the contract list setup and breakdown hours, loading access, parking, curfew, catering, alcohol, weather, and overtime terms?
  • Will every important approval be placed in writing?
  • What deposit and payment schedule does the venue require, and what are the cancellation and postponement terms?
  • What insurance certificates must each vendor provide, and when are they due to the venue?

Section 10 · For multicultural weddings

Questions for Multicultural Weddings

Ask only what matches your traditions, and name each one. “A cultural ceremony” is easy for a venue to agree to; “sacred fire, indoors, for forty minutes” gets a real answer.

iMultiple Ceremonies & Spaces

AskedConfirmed
  • Can the venue host two or more ceremonies, room transformations, rehearsals, and cultural leaders?
  • Is a prayer, preparation, or ceremonial holding room available?

iiSacred Fire & Ritual Materials

AskedConfirmed
  • Are sacred fire, incense, oil lamps, candles, smoke, rice, petals, powders, water, oils, and ceremonial foods permitted?
  • What fire-marshal approval, permits, ventilation, protective materials, insurance, cleanup, or staffing are required?

Notes

iiiProcessions & Grand Entrances

AskedConfirmed
  • Can the property support a baraat or other cultural procession, musicians, dancers, animals, or specialty transportation?
  • What route, staging, timing, noise, rain, parking, valet, permit, insurance, and security requirements apply?

ivAttire, Language & Family Needs

AskedConfirmed
  • Are there enough private rooms for attire changes, stylists, family, elders, performers, prayer, nursing, and quiet breaks?
  • Can programs, signs, screens, ceremonies, interpreters, and announcements use multiple languages?

vExtended Timeline & Guest Experience

AskedConfirmed
  • Is the rental window long enough for every ceremony, attire change, meal, performance, photo period, room flip, and rest break?
  • Where will guests wait during transitions, and can refreshments be served?
  • Can décor and ceremonial structures remain overnight with secure storage?

Venue Red Flags

  • “We’re flexible,” with no written detail behind it.
  • Only an hour or two to set up décor before guests arrive.
  • No one can tell you the freight-elevator or doorway measurements, or your décor will not fit.
  • The weather-backup space cannot hold your full guest count.
  • The quote leaves out service charges, tax, security, or overtime.
  • Guest parking or valet cannot handle your guest count.
  • Hanging installations, candles, or effects in your design are not permitted.
  • The venue will not put an approval in the contract.

The last step

Before You Sign
